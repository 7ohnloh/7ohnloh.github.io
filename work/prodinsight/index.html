The question

Where does AirPods Max fall behind?

Apple's AirPods Max competes with Sony's WH-1000XM5 and Sennheiser's Momentum 4. Reviews tell you what critics think. We wanted to know what owners say, feature by feature, and turn that into design opportunities a product team could act on.

My part · data collection

Getting comments that count

I built the scrapers for all three products. From YouTube we took up to 500 top-level comments per video, with their like counts. From Reddit we took the top posts for each product, including nested replies and vote scores.

Not every comment carries the same weight, so each one was scored on three things:

50%how strongly positive or negative it is
30%how many likes it got
20%how long it is, as a sign of substance

We cleaned the text lightly on purpose. "I love it!!!" says more than "I love it", and the sentiment model reads punctuation and emoji, so we only removed links and standardized capitalization.

Findings

Five features where it trails

We scored each comment that mentioned a feature as positive, neutral or negative, then compared AirPods Max with the average of its two rivals. Any gap above 0.2 counted as underperforming.

From numbers to ideas

Opportunities you can trace back

A gap tells you where, not why. We embedded every comment and indexed them with FAISS, so a search for "battery" finds "lasts for 30 hours on a single charge" even though the words don't match. For each weak feature, the 10 most relevant comments went to GPT-3.5, which pulled out pain points, unmet needs and suggestions.

Fit

Comfort for people with bigger ears, more adjustability, and more colour choices.

Latency

Confusion about "low latency" being marketed for a wired mode, and interest in a dual wired and wireless mode.

Microphone

Inconsistent call quality that makes the price feel hard to justify.

Because each idea comes from specific comments, a designer can read exactly what users said, like "hurts after 30 minutes" instead of a vague "uncomfortable".

Limitations

What the data can't tell you

  • Reddit leans technical and audiophile, and YouTube comments are shorter and more emotional. Shopping sites like Amazon and Shopee would add other voices, but their APIs were restricted.
  • TextBlob can miss sarcasm. We chose it over VADER because its continuous scores ranked how strong each comment was more precisely.
